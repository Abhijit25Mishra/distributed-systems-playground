## Findings

**The simulation and the closed form agree, which is the test worth having.**
A page waits on all `k` calls, so it exceeds a threshold when any single call
does: `1 - (1 - p)^k`. Measured against 2000 simulated pages at the p99
threshold:

```
  fan-out    slow pages    theory
        1          1.0%      1.0%
        5          5.0%      4.9%
       20         18.4%     18.2%
      100         63.8%     63.4%
      200         86.7%     86.6%
```

The test asserting these track within 3 points is the one that would catch the
model drifting from the thing it claims to be showing. It is the same shape as
the ring's "trace agrees with lookup for every key".

**The median never moves and that is the whole argument.** The tail control
moves p99 from 120ms to 4s while the median call stays pinned at 100ms. At
fan-out 100 the median *page* is 1.16s, which is slower than the p99 of a
single call. A dashboard showing averages would report a healthy service the
entire time.

**Log axes everywhere, including on the controls.** Latency spans four decades,
so the x axis is logarithmic or the median sits in the left edge. The same
argument applies to the sliders: fan-out matters most between 1 and 30, and a
linear 1-200 track gives that 15% of its length. This is the second time this
week that a linear slider turned out to be hiding the range that matters, after
the ring's key count. Worth treating as a default rather than a discovery next
time.

## What I got wrong / missed

- **I introduced an inverted-histogram bug while fixing a label collision.**
  At fan-out 100 the three percentile labels bunched up and ran together
  (`p50 1.16sp90 2.12s`), so I gave each its own row. That reserves a fixed
  39px, and the bar height is `rowHeight - 39`, which goes **negative below
  about a 200px canvas** and would draw the bars upward from the baseline.
  Caught by computing the height at five canvas sizes rather than by looking,
  which is the only reason it was caught at all: the browser extension had
  disconnected by then and I could not have seen it.
- **The percentile-label fix is not visually verified.** The browser extension
  dropped mid-session, so the stacked label rows and the floored bar height
  have been checked by construction and arithmetic, not by screenshot. Three
  labels on three distinct rows cannot overlap, and the floor cannot go
  negative, but "cannot by construction" is a weaker claim than "looked at it"
  and this project has a documented history of the screenshot finding what the
  code review missed.
- **I nearly shipped a colour contradiction.** The headline read "one call" in
  green and "one page" in orange, borrowed from the ring's good/bad churn
  styling, while the histogram and the swatches drew the same two entities in
  blue and orange. Colour has to follow the entity, not a verdict about it. The
  ring gets away with good/bad because its two rows are not series in the
  figure; here they are.
- **The write-up is agent-written prose on a personal site.** It is inside the
  boundary as written, and it is also the first thing on the site that speaks
  in the author's voice without being his sentences. Flagged rather than
  assumed.
- **Ch 2 remains open**, though for the first time the reason is not that the
  session went to code: the human is picking it up now and this item was
  handed over precisely so that could happen.
